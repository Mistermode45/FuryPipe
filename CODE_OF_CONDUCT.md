# FuryPipe Code of Conduct

## Purpose

FuryPipe is an engineering project built around reviewable evidence, security boundaries and collaborative problem solving. Project spaces should make it possible to disagree on technical decisions without making participation hostile or personal.

## Expected behavior

Contributors, maintainers and users participating in project spaces are expected to:

- discuss ideas and code rather than attacking people;
- give specific, actionable technical feedback;
- distinguish facts, measurements, assumptions and opinions;
- respect security and privacy boundaries;
- avoid publishing private prompts, credentials or identifying data;
- accept that evidence may change a technical conclusion;
- credit upstream work and contributors accurately;
- keep reviews focused on the change and its risks.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following is not acceptable in FuryPipe project spaces:

- harassment, threats or targeted personal attacks;
- discriminatory or demeaning language;
- deliberate exposure of private or sensitive information;
- publishing credentials, private prompts or security exploit details outside the approved reporting process;
- sustained disruption of issues, reviews or discussions;
- impersonation or intentionally misleading attribution;
- retaliation against good-faith security reporters or reviewers.

## Technical disagreement

Strong disagreement is allowed. Keep it technical.

When evidence conflicts, prefer reproducible tests, source references and clearly scoped claims. Maintainers may reject a contribution without rejecting the contributor.

## Security reports

Potential vulnerabilities must follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) and GitHub private vulnerability reporting.

Do not use public issues for undisclosed vulnerabilities.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, hide, lock or remove contributions that violate this policy and may restrict participation when necessary to protect the project or its participants.

Enforcement decisions should be proportionate to severity, repetition and impact. Good-faith mistakes should normally be corrected before stronger action is considered.

## Scope

This policy applies to FuryPipe repository issues, pull requests, reviews and other project-managed collaboration spaces.
